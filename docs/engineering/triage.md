## What it does

`triage` moves requests on the configured Work Tracker through a small **state machine** of triage roles — categorise them, verify the claim, grill them into shape if needed, and leave a complete executable Agent Brief.

It never labels blind. Every triaged item carries exactly one **category** role (`bug` / `enhancement`) and one **state** role (`needs-triage`, `needs-info`, `ready-for-agent`, `ready-for-human`, `wontfix`). Before `ready-for-agent`, it verifies the claim and posts a source-authorized brief with What to build, Acceptance criteria, Repository References, non-empty Repository Scope, Context Scope, Cross-Repository Seams, Out of scope, and Blocked by.

## When to reach for it

You invoke this by typing `/triage` and describing what you want in natural language — the agent won't reach for it on its own. "Show me anything that needs my attention", "let's look at #42", "move #42 to ready-for-agent".

Reach for it when your Work Tracker has raw, unevaluated requests and you want them sorted, verified, and turned into work an agent or human can pick up. To turn settled planning into a fresh specification, use [to-spec](https://aihero.dev/skills-to-spec); to split one into delivery tickets, use [to-tickets](https://aihero.dev/skills-to-tickets).

## Prerequisites

`triage` reads and writes the configured Work Tracker and follows configured Domain Orientation. The applicable setup skill must provide the tracker and Routing Label mapping first. The binding also decides whether external Review Proposals count as a request surface and who counts as external.

## A Review Proposal is a request with attached code

Where the binding treats external Review Proposals as a request surface, `triage` runs them through the same machine. `ready-for-agent` means the complete brief exists and an agent should take the next step on the code; `ready-for-human` means it is ready for human action.

## Verify before you brief

The step that separates `triage` from ad-hoc labelling is verification. It reproduces the bug from the reporter's steps, or checks out the Review Proposal and runs the tests, and reports back: confirmed with a code path, failed, or insufficient detail. It also runs two codebase checks — **redundancy** (is this already implemented?) and **prior rejection** (does `.out-of-scope/` already say no?).

## It's working if

- Every item it touches ends with exactly one category role and one state role — never zero, never two conflicting states.
- It hands you a recommendation with reasoning and waits, rather than relabelling on its own.
- Bugs get reproduced and Review Proposals get run before anything reaches `ready-for-agent`.
- Direct maintainer overrides can skip grilling but never the complete Agent Brief or its post-publication state check.
- Every comment it posts to the tracker opens with the `> *This was generated by AI during triage.*` disclaimer.

## Where it fits

`triage` is the periodic maintenance pass over the Work Tracker. When a request needs sharpening it leans on [grilling](https://aihero.dev/skills-grilling) and [domain-modeling](https://aihero.dev/skills-domain-modeling), preserving future-state changes in planning authority until their owners are authorized to write them. Its close neighbour is [to-spec](https://aihero.dev/skills-to-spec), which publishes settled planning sources. When you're unsure which skill or flow fits, [ask-matt](https://aihero.dev/skills-ask-matt) routes you.
